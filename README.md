# Teaching Materials for Discrete Methods in Modern AI

This repository contains the teaching materials for the "Discrete Methods in Modern AI" course given at the University of Tübingen (Winter Semester 2026/2027).

### Course Description 

In this course, we examine applications of discrete modeling techniques to problems in modern language modeling and generative AI. We will study how such techniques—drawn from logic, automated reasoning, and classical probabilistic inference—can support deeper semantic analysis of current systems, provide a means of incorporating structured knowledge and constraints into learning and inference, and offer theoretical tools for characterizing their capabilities and limitations. More specifically, we will introduce methods for [SAT solving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_satisfiability_problem) and related problems (e.g., propositional model counting), [tractable logical and probabilistic inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.02942), and [probabilistic programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probabilistic_programming), focusing on their applications to next-token modeling, learning with semantic constraints, preference-based post-training, prompting, and test-time inference.

The lectures and accompanying materials given here will provide a self-contained presentation of the core course content, drawing on research papers from the field. Students will be expected to engage with assigned readings each week, which will provide additional depth and exposure to the original research. The course will have a hands-on component: programming exercises will be provided that allow students to implement the methods discussed in class and develop a concrete understanding of how they work. Course evaluation will largely be project-based: students will be asked to pursue research projects that dig deeper in the core course content.

**Requirements**: This course is intended for advanced bachelor/master students in [ISCL](https://uni-tuebingen.de/en/faculties/faculty-of-humanities/departments/modern-languages/department-of-linguistics/courses-of-study/courses-of-study-at-the-sfs/international-studies-in-computational-linguistics/ba-programme-iscl/), as well as students in both the [cognitive science](https://uni-tuebingen.de/en/faculties/faculty-of-humanities/departments/modern-languages/department-of-linguistics/courses-of-study/courses-of-study-at-the-sfs/international-studies-in-computational-linguistics/ba-programme-iscl/) and [machine learning](https://uni-tuebingen.de/fakultaeten/mathematisch-naturwissenschaftliche-fakultaet/fachbereiche/informatik/studium/studierende/lehre-studienorganisation/studiengaenge/machine-learning/) master programs. It requires knowledge of Python, basic machine learning (e.g., deep learning) and discrete mathematics (e.g., classical logic and discrete probability). Knowledge of language modeling is not strictly required, we will go through the basics together through concrete examples. We will also cover the more advanced discrete modeling techniques in a self-contained manner.
